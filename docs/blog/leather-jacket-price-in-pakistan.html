The short answer

A full genuine leather jacket made in Pakistan costs Rs 20,000 to Rs 40,000. Anything meaningfully below that is either a blend, a very thin hide, or not leather at all.

Price What you are almost certainly buying
Under Rs 8,000 PU, rexine or bonded leather. Convincing on the hanger, cracked and peeling within two winters.
Rs 8,000 – 15,000 A blend, a thin or small hide, or a genuine jacket on heavy discount. Ask what the shell is and get it in writing.
Rs 20,000 – 40,000 The honest band for a full genuine leather jacket made locally, properly lined and stitched.
Above Rs 45,000 Usually imported, or a designer margin. Not necessarily better leather.

An expensive jacket is not automatically real. But a cheap one almost certainly is not, and that asymmetry is the whole point of knowing the bands.

Where the money actually goes

A finished jacket takes roughly 25 to 35 square feet of hide. At Pakistani tannery rates for sheepskin of a grade you would want against your skin, the leather alone runs well into five figures before anyone has cut a pattern.

Then, in rough order of cost:

  • Cutting. Hides are irregular. A cutter works around thin patches, scars and neck creases, and wastes some of every skin doing it. Cheaper jackets skip this by using the whole hide and putting weak leather in places you will find out about later.
  • Stitching. A biker jacket is thirty-odd panels. Every seam through leather is permanent — a needle hole cannot be unpicked and re-sewn invisibly the way it can in cloth.
  • Lining and interlining. The part nobody photographs and everybody feels. A jacket that is cold, clammy or noisy is usually lined badly rather than made of bad leather.
  • Hardware. Zips, snaps and buckles. A branded zip costs several times a generic one and is the single most common failure point on a cheap jacket.

None of that scales down gracefully. To hit Rs 8,000 you cannot buy a smaller amount of leather — you have to stop buying leather.

What Rs 22,000 buys against Rs 35,000

This is the question no one in this market answers, because answering it means publishing your own ladder. Ours runs from Rs 22,000 to Rs 35,000 across 30 jackets, and every one of them is genuine top-grain sheep leather. So the difference is not the material. It is this:

Band Jackets What moves the price
Rs 22,000 – 25,000 6 Cleaner cuts with fewer panels — classic bikers and cafe racers. Least leather, least labour, and the easiest place to start.
Rs 25,000 – 28,000 12 Where most of the range sits. More panelling, quilting, zip detailing or a waxed finish — more hours rather than more hide.
Rs 28,000 – 30,000 5 Heavier construction and more involved finishing, including distressed and vintage treatments that are applied by hand after the jacket is built.
Rs 32,000 – 35,000 7 Longer cuts and added materials. A double-breasted coat needs noticeably more hide than a cropped biker; a fur collar is a second material bought, matched and set by hand.

The useful takeaway is not our ladder specifically. It is that within genuine leather, price tracks hide area, panel count and finishing hours — not quality of leather. A Rs 22,000 jacket from an honest maker is not made of worse leather than their Rs 35,000 one. It is a simpler jacket. If a seller cannot explain their own spread in those terms, the spread is margin.

Sheep or cow, and why it changes the price

Almost every fashion leather jacket in Pakistan is sheep leather, ours included. It is lighter, softer, and it drapes close to the body instead of standing away from it. Cow leather is heavier and far more abrasion-resistant, which is why motorcycle gear uses it and why it costs more to buy and more to work — it is harder on needles, harder to cut cleanly, and slower to sew.

Neither is better in the abstract. For everyday winter wear, sheep is the more comfortable jacket. If you actually ride, ask for cow and expect to pay more for it. Made to order, and what that does to the price

We do not hold finished jackets. Each one is cut after the order comes in, to the size you choose, which has two consequences worth being straight about.

It removes the cost of guessing. A brand holding stock has to predict how many XLs in black will sell, and prices in the ones that do not. It also removes the compromise that ruins most leather jackets, which is fit — a jacket that fits badly does not get worn, whatever it cost.

What it costs you is flexibility. Jackets are prepaid only, no cash on delivery, because we cannot absorb a refused Rs 30,000 piece that was cut to one person's measurements. And you wait, because production starts when you order. If you need a jacket this weekend, buy one that already exists.

Is it worth it? The only arithmetic that matters

A leather jacket lasts a decade or more with basic care. Work it through: a Rs 27,000 jacket worn forty times a winter for five winters is about Rs 135 a wear, and it is still a jacket at the end of it. A Rs 7,000 synthetic replaced every second winter is roughly the same money over the same decade and leaves you with nothing.

That maths only holds if you wear it, which brings it back to fit and to honest material. Both of those are worth paying for. Neither is worth paying extra for.

What to ask before you pay

  1. What is the shell made of? Sheep, cow, goat, or a blend — and get the answer in writing. An honest seller answers in one line. A page that avoids naming the material has told you the answer.
  2. Is that price for the finished jacket in my size? Watch for prices quoted against the smallest size, with the larger ones costing more.
  3. What happens if it does not fit? On a made-to-order jacket the answer should involve remaking, not a restocking fee.
  4. Who repairs it if a zip fails in year three? Ours carries a lifetime craftsmanship warranty — if stitching or hardware fails in normal use we repair or replace it. Ask anyone else the same question.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a leather jacket cost in Pakistan?

A genuine leather jacket made in Pakistan costs roughly Rs 20,000 to Rs 40,000. Our own range runs Rs 22,000 to Rs 35,000 across 30 styles, all genuine sheep leather. Below about Rs 8,000, you are almost certainly buying PU or a bonded blend whatever the label says.

Why are some leather jackets Rs 25,000 and others Rs 35,000?

Within one honest maker the difference is hide area, panel count and finishing hours — not leather quality. A longer or double-breasted cut uses noticeably more hide than a cropped biker, and additions like a fur collar or a hand-applied distressed finish add materials and labour. If a seller cannot explain their spread in those terms, the difference is margin.

What is the price of an original leather jacket in Pakistan?

The same Rs 20,000 to Rs 40,000 band. “Original” is not a grade, so it does not carry its own price — ask instead which hide it is and how it was finished, because those do.

Is a cheap leather jacket ever real leather?

Occasionally, on genuine clearance. But the raw hide for one jacket runs into five figures on its own, so a new jacket priced under Rs 8,000 cannot be full leather and cover its own materials. Treat it as synthetic unless the seller will name the hide in writing.

Does a leather jacket cost more in a larger size?

It costs us more to make — a XXXL uses meaningfully more hide than a S. We price every size the same anyway, across the full S to XXXL range. Not everyone does, so it is worth checking before you assume the advertised price is your price.

Why is cow leather priced differently from sheep?

Cow hide costs more to buy and more to work — it is thicker, harder to cut cleanly and slower to stitch. We make cow versions to order and quote them per jacket rather than listing one price, because the difference depends on the cut.

The bottom line

Pakistan makes very good leather, and sells a great deal of plastic labelled as leather. The price bands above are the fastest way to tell which one you are looking at, before you have touched the jacket. Check the number, ask what the shell is, and then buy the one that fits.
