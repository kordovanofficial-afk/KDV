# BRIEF — `sizing-regret`

**Kordovan · September 2026 jacket campaign · backup ad**

---

## Objective

Cold prospecting in the **northern cities only**. Reach the man about to buy a leather
jacket in September and put one specific, checkable thought in his head before he does:
*the jacket you try on this month has to close over a sweater in January.*

Landing page: `https://kordovanleather.com/collections/mens-leather-jackets`

## The one thing the viewer should feel

*"That has happened to me — or it is about to."*

A small, precise, recognisable regret. Not fear, not urgency. The ad's whole job is to
make a fit problem visible **before** it is bought into, and then point at made-to-order
as the boring, obvious answer. If a viewer's only takeaway is "measure over the sweater",
the ad has still done useful work for the brand.

## The reading I took

The brief could be run two ways: *your old jacket does not fit any more* (regret,
past tense) or *the jacket you are about to buy will not fit in four months* (foresight,
future tense). **I took the second.** It is the more specific film — a September buyer
looking at a September fit is a real person with a real decision in the next fortnight,
and it lets the ad end on a solvable problem rather than a sunk cost. The first reading
ends with a man who already wasted his money and has no reason to trust the next seller.

## Geography — and why this ad is not for Karachi

This runs in **Lahore · Islamabad · Rawalpindi**. Not Karachi.

Karachi is a **style** market, not a warmth market. The winter is mild; nobody there is
layering a wool sweater under a leather jacket in January, and a film built entirely on
that premise is inert or faintly ridiculous to a Karachi viewer. It would also be
spending the northern half of the budget on people the argument cannot reach.

This is a recommendation against the shared brief, which named all four cities, and it
is worth the split: **Karachi gets `leather-test`**, whose authenticity angle is
season-independent and which carries the highest jacket AOV market on an argument that
actually lands there. If the four-city ad set has to stay consolidated for learning-phase
reasons at PKR 1,000/day, run `leather-test` as the consolidated creative and hold
`sizing-regret` for the October ramp when budget can support a northern ad set of its own.

## Season timing

PK jacket impressions run flat through September (13.6k, identical to August) and then
roughly triple in October (35k) and again in November (78.6k). **September is not the
month to spend into this angle at volume** — it is the month to get the creative tested
and the learning done so October opens with a proven asset. Treat any September result
here as a signal read, not a scaling decision.

⚠️ August 2026 is tracking **64% below** August 2025 with average position drifting 10.9
→ 14.3, cause not yet established. Diagnose that before committing real season budget.

## FILM / GENERATE split

**0 shots FILM · 5 shots GENERATE · 15.0 seconds**

This is a fully synthetic production, and it is legitimately fully synthetic — which is
worth stating precisely, because the reasoning is what makes it safe to repeat.

### Why every shot may be generated here

The integrity rule bites on shots whose persuasive job is to **prove something about the
physical product**: grain under a thumb, pore structure, a cut edge, a stitch. This ad
makes no such claim. Its entire argument is about **a garment's relationship to a body in
two seasons** — a t-shirt in September, a sweater in January — and about a commercial
fact (made to order) that is carried in the caption, not demonstrated in the picture.

Everything on screen is mood, weather, environment and wardrobe-on-body: a warm evening
street, a cold hill road, mist, a wool sweater, a man's shoulders. None of it testifies
that any leather is real. Nothing here is evidence.

### The one shot that sits closest to the line — shot 04

Shot 04 shows a well-cut jacket closing cleanly over a thick sweater. Read carelessly,
that is a demonstration of a product capability, and a generated demonstration of a
product capability is exactly what the integrity rule forbids.

Two directions keep it on the right side, and both are binding:

1. **No measurement beat.** No tape measure, no on-screen figure, no before/after
   comparison, no "look, it closes" gesture. The shot is a man walking, comfortable. The
   moment it stages a proof, it becomes evidence and it has to be filmed.
2. **The claim lives in the caption, not the frame.** The caption says *"Made to order in
   Karachi. Tell us there's a sweater under it."* That is an **offer**, and it is
   underwritten by a fact (the jackets are made to order), not by the footage. The picture
   sets a mood; the sentence makes the promise.

> 🔴 **This one needs the owner's confirmation before it runs.** *"Tell us there's a
> sweater under it"* commits the workshop to actually honouring a chest-allowance request
> at order time. Made-to-order makes that plausible, but plausible is not the same as
> agreed. **Confirm the workshop will do it, and that whoever answers WhatsApp knows to
> ask the question, before this ad goes live.** If they will not, the caption drops back
> to the unarguable `Made to order in Karachi.` and the ad still works — slightly softer.

### If you want to strengthen it later

Swapping shot 04 for real footage — one filmed shot of an actual Kordovan jacket closing
over an actual sweater — makes the strongest beat in the ad load-bearing rather than
suggestive, and flips `self_ai_disclosure` considerations for that shot. It is not
required. It is the obvious first upgrade if this creative wins.

### Product identity

**No caption in this ad names a cut, a colour or a model.** That is deliberate: under the
label-matches-subject rule, naming one would oblige the generated jacket to *be* that
jacket, which no generator can honestly deliver. By keeping the copy at the range level
("made to order in Karachi", "from Rs 22,000") the film stays truthful about a collection
rather than making a claim about a specific SKU it never shows.

Corollary, and it matters: **the failing jacket in shots 02 and 03 must not be dressed as
a Kordovan cut.** It is a plain, unbranded, anonymous slim jacket. We are naming a
category mistake, not staging our own product failing.

### The structural device

The opening sentence is **split across the first cut**: `The jacket that fits you in
September` (0.0–3.0) → `won't close in January.` (3.0–5.8). A viewer who reads the first
half has to stay for the second to get a complete thought. It costs nothing and it buys
the three seconds where most cold viewers leave — which is exactly what "earn the second
before identifying as an ad" means in practice.

## Grade

Warm even when the weather is cold. Grey mist, not blue mist. Espresso shadows, never
black, never a cool cast. The temperature contrast between shot 01 (low golden sun) and
shots 02–05 (flat overcast) does the seasonal storytelling; the *grade* stays on the
warm side of neutral throughout so it still reads as Kordovan.

## Faces

No face is fully visible in any shot. Every framing crops above the collarbone, turns the
subject away, or backlights him into silhouette. This is not squeamishness — identity
drift between generated clips is the single most reliable tell that footage is synthetic,
and the cheapest fix is to never show the same face twice.

## Generation target

LTX-2.3 (Apache 2.0) on a free Colab T4 or via Wan2GP. Five clips, 704×1280, 97 frames at
24fps (4.04s each), trimmed to shot length and upscaled to 1080×1920 at assembly. Every
clip is under 5 seconds and under 720p-class resolution — the whole ad is five generations
on free-tier hardware.
