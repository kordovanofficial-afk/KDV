<h2>What genuine leather shoes actually cost in Pakistan</h2>

<p>Most lists skip this part, and it is the part that protects you.</p>

<p>A pair of men's shoes needs only a few square feet of upper leather, so the hide is a smaller share of the cost than it is in a jacket. The money goes into what you cannot see in a photo: whether the lining is leather or fabric, whether the sole is leather, rubber or moulded plastic, how the sole is attached, and how much handwork the upper gets. That is why two black oxfords that look identical online can be Rs 4,000 and Rs 24,000, and both can honestly be called "leather shoes".</p>

<p>A practical guide:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Under Rs 5,000:</strong> expect a synthetic upper, or a split leather so heavily coated that it behaves like plastic. It looks sharp at first, then cracks across the toe crease within a season or two.</li>
<li><strong>Rs 5,000 – 10,000:</strong> often a genuine leather upper with a synthetic or fabric lining and a moulded sole, factory-made in volume. This can be good value for daily wear. Check the lining.</li>
<li><strong>Rs 10,000 – 20,000:</strong> leather upper and leather lining from an established brand, usually with a glued (cemented) sole. This is the top line of most high-street ranges.</li>
<li><strong>Rs 20,000 – 35,000:</strong> made-to-order or small-batch handcrafted pairs, with more handwork on the upper and more choice of leather and finish.</li>
<li><strong>Above Rs 35,000:</strong> usually imported, or a designer margin. That does not automatically mean better leather.</li>
</ul>

<p>None of this means an expensive pair is automatically good. It means a very cheap pair almost certainly is not what it claims. If a seller cannot tell you what the upper, the lining and the sole are made of, that tells you enough.</p>

<h2>How to check the leather yourself</h2>

<figure style="margin:24px 0;">
<img src="{{IMG_GRAIN}}" sizes="(max-width: 768px) 100vw, 820px" width="1563" height="1006" alt="Close-up of the toe crease on a brown leather shoe showing fine natural creasing" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="width:100%;height:auto;border-radius:8px;">
<figcaption style="font-size:14px;color:#8B8174;margin-top:8px;">How genuine leather creases at the toe: fine, soft wrinkles rather than one sharp fold.</figcaption>
</figure>

<p>Five checks you can do in a shop, or the day a parcel arrives:</p>

<ol>
<li><strong>Bend it at the toe crease.</strong> Real leather forms fine, soft wrinkles. A coated or synthetic upper folds in one hard line, and that is where it will crack.</li>
<li><strong>Look inside.</strong> A leather lining has a soft, slightly fibrous back. Fabric or a smooth plastic film means the lining is synthetic, even if the upper is leather.</li>
<li><strong>Check the edges.</strong> Look where the upper meets the sole and at any cut edge. Leather shows fibres. Synthetic shows a woven fabric backing.</li>
<li><strong>Read the sole.</strong> Leather soles are stamped or have a visible grain and a stitched or neatly finished edge. Moulded soles show a seam line from the mould.</li>
<li><strong>Do not trust the stamp alone.</strong> "Genuine leather" printed on the insole tells you very little. Ask what each part is made of, and get the answer in writing if you are buying online.</li>
</ol>

<p>For a fuller checklist that also covers wallets and bags, read <a href="/blogs/news/real-vs-fake-leather-how-to-check">Real vs Fake Leather: 8 Ways to Check Before You Buy</a>.</p>

<h2>The ten brands</h2>

<h3>10. Metro</h3>
<p>Metro is best known for women's footwear, and its men's range follows the same approach: current styles, accessible prices and plenty of mall stores. It is a sensible place to pick up a smart pair for an event without spending much.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out for:</strong> the range mixes leather and synthetic uppers, so read the material on each pair rather than assuming.</p>

<h3>9. Servis</h3>
<p>Servis is one of the most widely available names in the country, built on value and reach. It is dependable for school, office and everyday pairs at prices most families can manage.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out for:</strong> the formal leather range is narrower than the casual one. Check the lining and sole if you want a pair that lasts years rather than a season.</p>

<h3>8. Bata</h3>
<p>Bata has a store in almost every city and sizing you can try on the spot. Its everyday formal pairs are reliable, and exchanges are easy because there is always a branch nearby.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out for:</strong> at the lower end of the range, uppers and soles are often synthetic. Look for the leather-upper lines if leather is what you want.</p>

<h3>7. Charcoal</h3>
<p>Charcoal is a menswear house rather than a shoemaker, and its shoes are designed to sit with its tailoring. If you buy a suit there, the matching pair makes the whole look easy.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out for:</strong> footwear is a side line, so the choice is smaller than at a shoe specialist.</p>

<h3>6. Insignia</h3>
<p>Insignia is fashion-led, with formal and casual pairs for men and women that follow the season closely. It is good if you care more about the look than about decades of wear.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out for:</strong> trend shapes date faster than plain oxfords and loafers. Check the material line on each product.</p>

<h3>5. Hush Puppies</h3>
<p>Hush Puppies is an international comfort brand. Its pairs are soft, cushioned and easy on the feet from the first wear, which suits men who stand or walk all day.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out for:</strong> the comfort focus means chunkier soles and softer shapes. They are less sharp with a suit or sherwani than a sleek leather-soled oxford.</p>

<h3>4. Urban Sole</h3>
<p>Urban Sole is one of the few high-street names that puts leather footwear at the centre of its range rather than at the edge. Its shapes are modern and its formal choice is wide.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out for:</strong> fits vary between lasts, so try the exact style in a store, or check the size chart carefully online.</p>

<h3>3. Borjan</h3>
<p>Borjan is strong on formal and wedding shoes, with a large store network and frequent new designs. It is a practical choice when you need a dressy pair for a specific occasion and want to try it on first.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out for:</strong> occasion styles with heavy detailing get worn less after the event. If you want one pair for years, choose its plainer designs.</p>

<h3>2. ECS</h3>
<p>ECS is one of the longest-standing names in Pakistani formal leather shoes. Its classic office shoes have a loyal following among men who simply rebuy the same style.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out for:</strong> the styling is conservative on purpose. If you want something with more character, look elsewhere.</p>

<li><strong>Brogue:</strong> an oxford or derby with decorative perforations. It is slightly less formal, so it works with trousers and a blazer as well as a suit.</li>
<li><strong>Wholecut:</strong> cut from a single piece of leather with almost no seams. It is the sleekest shape and shows the leather best, so it suits evening wear.</li>
<li><strong>Monk strap:</strong> a buckle instead of laces. It is as smart as an oxford and easier to slip on and off, which helps at mosques, homes and weddings.</li>
<li><strong>Loafer and moccasin:</strong> no laces, soft construction, the most comfortable for long days. It works with shalwar kameez as well as chinos.</li>
</ul>

<p>Match your belt to your shoes: same colour family, similar finish. Our <a href="/collections/mens-leather-belts">men's leather belts</a> come in black and browns chosen with that in mind.</p>

<h2>Glued or stitched? What the sole tells you</h2>

</figure>

<p>Most shoes sold in Pakistan at every price have glued, or cemented, soles. There is nothing wrong with that: it keeps shoes light and flexible, and a well-made cemented shoe lasts years. Stitched constructions cost more and can usually be resoled, which matters if you plan to wear one pair for a decade. If a brand says a shoe is stitched, look for visible stitching around the sole edge, and ask whether a cobbler can resole it.</p>

<h2>Buying for Pakistani weather</h2>

<p>Heat and the monsoon are harder on shoes than cold is. In summer, a leather lining helps your feet breathe far better than a synthetic one, and that is worth paying for. In the monsoon, wet leather soles wear quickly and grip poorly, so keep a rubber-soled pair for rainy commutes. In the winters of Lahore, Islamabad and the north, any well-polished leather pair does well. Condition the leather before the season so dry air and dust do not crack it.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>What is the best leather shoe brand in Pakistan?</h3>
<p>It depends on what you need. For a formal pair made to your size, we would argue for Kordovan, though we are not neutral. For trying shoes on in a shop with easy exchanges, ECS, Borjan or Urban Sole. For all-day comfort, Hush Puppies. For a low-priced everyday pair, Bata or Servis.</p>

<h3>How much should genuine leather shoes cost in Pakistan?</h3>
<p>A factory-made pair with a genuine leather upper usually starts around Rs 5,000 to Rs 10,000. Leather upper and leather lining from an established brand usually sit between Rs 10,000 and Rs 20,000. Made-to-order and small-batch handcrafted pairs usually cost Rs 20,000 to Rs 35,000. Below about Rs 5,000, expect a synthetic or heavily coated upper, whatever the box says.</p>

<h3>How can I tell if shoes are real leather?</h3>
<p>Bend the shoe at the toe crease: real leather forms fine, soft wrinkles, while a coated or synthetic upper folds in sharp lines. Look inside: a leather lining has a fibrous, suede-like back. Check the raw edges and the smell. A stamp that says "genuine leather" on its own proves very little, so ask what the upper, lining and sole are each made of.</p>

<h3>Leather sole or rubber sole for Pakistan?</h3>
<p>A leather sole breathes and looks sharper, which suits offices, weddings and dry winters. A rubber sole grips better on wet roads and wears more slowly, which suits the monsoon and daily commuting. Many men keep one of each.</p>

<h3>How do I make leather shoes last longer?</h3>
<p>Use cedar or plain shoe trees so the shape holds and moisture is drawn out. Do not wear the same pair two days running. Wipe off dust, condition the leather every few months and polish it to the colour. If the shoes get wet, stuff them with newspaper and let them dry away from heat. Our <a href="/pages/leather-care-guide">leather care guide</a> covers the details.</p>

<h2>The bottom line</h2>

<p>Pakistan makes and sells a great many good leather shoes, and a great many coated imitations that look the same in a photo. Whichever brand you choose from this list, ask what the upper, lining and sole are made of, check the price against the bands above, and buy the shape you will actually wear. A pair that sits in the box does not get better with age. A pair you wear and look after does.</p>
